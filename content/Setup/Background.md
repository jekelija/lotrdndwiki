# The Fourth (or Fifth?) Age of Middle-earth

Hundreds of years have passed since Frodo destroyed the One Ring. Kings and Queens have risen and fallen and risen and fallen again.

No king has been strong enough to unify the land in some time, and Orc and Goblin raids have become more frequent and more powerful, uniting under a powerful leader who calls himself **Eluine**.

The Dwarves hide deep in the mountains and hills to the North, rarely interacting with humankind.

The Elves have all but left Middle-earth, with **Celeborn** and **Círdan the Shipwright** preparing the Last Ship to the Undying Lands.

The resonance of the **Music of the Ainur** has faded; what magic remains is a far cry from the magic of old. But some magic remains.

---

## Legends and Folklore

Sauron, Frodo, Gandalf, and their ilk have passed into **myth and folklore**.

Sauron is little more than a boogeyman meant to frighten young children:

> “Go to bed or Sauron will get you.”  
> “Eat your vegetables or Sauron will come tonight.”

---

## The Chain of Power

Recently, **archaeologists unearthed documents from Isengard** detailing **Saruman’s attempts to replicate the power contained in the One Ring**, reigniting a flurry of interest in the Third Age.

They believe that Saruman **succeeded in creating a chain of great power** — perhaps not an equal to the One Ring, but nearly so.

> “He (Saruman) has long studied the arts of the Enemy himself. He is not content to be a servant; he would be a master in his own right. And he believes that it is only by matching Sauron’s power that he can hope to defeat him. Hence his desire to find the One Ring and, if he could not find it, to make one himself.”  
> — _J.R.R. Tolkien_

Different powers and factions interpret this discovery in their own way:

- Some wish to **acquire** the chain for its historical significance.
- Others wish to **harness** its power.
- And still others wish to **destroy** it before it falls into the wrong hands.

**However**

And this is where we get real deep into Tolkien canon, so bear with me. Magic flows through persons, not through ambient fields. So the "fading of magic" is really the withdrawing of immortal presence. Magic is fading in the land, but not gone. So Saruman's chain, then, if it exists, isn't a battery of energy — it's a crystallized echo of Maia will. His essence lingers, unintentionally anchoring a spiritual frequency that otherwise would have gone silent.

There is concern among some parties that destruction of this ring would result in the permanent departure of magic from the land.

---

## Canon Notes

Saruman, while powerful, was much less powerful than Sauron, and therefore any artefact of power he created would also be less powerful.
But! All the Istar, Maiar, and most of the elves are gone, leaving this less powerful (hypothetical) artefact still the most powerful thing in the land.
