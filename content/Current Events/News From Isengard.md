_Recovered from the lower vaults of Orthanc, Isengard — Fourth Age, Year 421_

---

## The Herald of the West — Special Chronicle

Word has reached even the far corners of Rohan and Gondor of a most curious discovery within the blackened ruins of **Orthanc**, the once-fortified tower of Saruman the White. A company of scholars and delvers from Minas Tirith, working under royal charter, claim to have unearthed a **tattered parchment scroll**, sealed within a corroded chest buried deep beneath the tower’s foundations. The scroll, though faded by centuries, bears markings unmistakably **Elvish in form yet twisted by another hand**, alongside intricate diagrams of a **crown wrought with runes of dominion**. The learned believe it may represent the lost work of Saruman himself — a design for a **“Crown of Power”**, an artifice by which he sought to rival the Great Ring of Sauron in the dark years before his fall.

Those who have seen the scroll whisper of its strange beauty — silver filigree sketched in delicate spirals, ancient Sindarin phrases marred by Black Speech insertions, and margin notes in Saruman’s own spidery script. Yet most troubling of all is a **scrap of paper found beside it**, scarcely larger than a man’s hand, upon which a single passage remains legible. Some claim it is a message from one of his lesser servants, reporting that _“the Crown is complete, though it hums with will of its own.”_ Others insist it is a **personal note from Saruman**, addressed to no named recipient, declaring only: _“The Voice endures. The Crown shall speak when I cannot.”_

Whether this artifact truly marks the culmination of Saruman’s final madness or is but the delusion of his last, broken years remains a matter of fierce debate. The scholars have borne it hence to Minas Tirith for study, though many a man mutters that such things were better left buried — for even now, when the White Hand is long forgotten, its shadow may yet stir in secret places.
