# 🌍 Middle-earth: The Fourth Age, Year 400

> _“The Shadow has passed — yet the cracks it left have never truly closed.”_

Four centuries have passed since the **War of the Ring** and the destruction of the **One Ring** in the fires of Mount Doom. The age of great kings and dark lords has given way to an age of men — fractured, faded, and restless. Though peace endured for generations under the reign of **Elessar Telcontar (Aragorn II)**, time has eroded the unity he built. His kingdom, once spanning from the **White Mountains to the North Downs**, is now splintered into smaller realms and city-states, each claiming to be the rightful heir of Gondor’s legacy.

Magic has grown thin, and the memory of the Elder Days lies buried beneath centuries of dust and rumor. Yet the old world has not wholly vanished.

---

## ⚜️ The Realms of Men

### **The Fractured Kingdoms**

After the death of King Eldarion, son of Aragorn, the throne of Gondor passed through a line of lesser rulers who failed to hold the realm together. The **Reunited Kingdom** of Gondor and Arnor is now a memory.

Today, **five major realms** contend for influence across the former lands of Men:

- **Gondor Proper** – Centered on **Minas Tirith**, still a shining city but diminished in glory. The Steward-Kings claim direct descent from Eldarion but rule through council and compromise.
- **Anórien** – Once a province, now an independent realm that guards the White Mountains and the Anduin crossings. Known for its knights and its suspicion of foreign sorcery.
- **Rhovanion (The Free Marches)** – A confederation of northern lords descended from the riders of Rohan and scattered Dúnedain bloodlines. Fierce, honorable, and distrustful of the south.
- **Harondor** – A borderland between the fading strength of Gondor and the restless tribes of Harad. It thrives on trade, espionage, and shifting alliances.
- **Pelargir** – A maritime republic that broke away a century ago. It controls much of the southern coast and wields vast power through its navy and merchant fleets.

Each realm claims a portion of Aragorn’s legacy, yet none possess the unity or wisdom that once held the kingdoms together.

---

## 🌿 The Elves

The Elves are nearly gone from Middle-earth. Most sailed West long ago, though a few ancient kindreds remain in the hidden places of the world.

- **Lothlórien** lies empty and overgrown, its light extinguished.
- **Rivendell** remains, tended by a few of Elrond’s kin — wise, silent, and suspicious of mortals.
- In **Mirkwood**, now called **Eryn Lasgalen**, a small host of Wood-elves endures under **King Thranduil’s descendants**, though they rarely leave their forest.

Elves are now creatures of legend to most Men — glimpsed only in stories, songs, or dreams.

---

## ⚒️ The Dwarves

The **Dwarves of Durin’s Folk** remain the most enduring of the Free Peoples. They have reoccupied **Khazad-dûm (Moria)** after long centuries of cleansing and rebuilding, though parts of its depths remain sealed or haunted.

- The kingdom under the mountain of **Erebor** continues to prosper, forging trade links with Dale and the northern realms of Men.
- A newer stronghold, **Barazdûm**, stands in the southern Ered Nimrais — a mark of Dwarven ambition to reclaim lost glories.

Dwarves still whisper of the old alliances, but few venture far. Their crafts are rarer now, their halls quieter, their songs more solemn.

---

## 🌾 The Hobbits

In the far West of Eriador, beyond the ruins of the old North Kingdom, lies the **Shire** — peaceful, hidden, and mostly forgotten by the outside world.

Most hobbits remember little of their great deeds. Bilbo, Frodo, and Samwise are now ancient legends, spoken of in fireside tales that even the tellers barely believe. Few Shirefolk travel far; fewer still realize that the world beyond their borders grows darker once again.

---

## 🌲 The Ents and the Wild

The **Ents** endure, though their numbers dwindle. Fangorn Forest has grown vast, merging with the southern Greenwood and creeping eastward toward the Brown Lands.

There are tales of new trees that move without roots and of shadows beneath the canopy where even Elves will not tread. Some say the Entwives have returned; others whisper that what now stirs in the woods is something older, colder, and less forgiving.

---

## ⚔️ The Orcs and Goblins

Though Sauron fell and the great hosts of Mordor were broken, **Orcs and Goblins** were never fully destroyed.

Centuries of hiding in the **Misty Mountains**, **Mordor’s ash-plains**, and the **Ephel Dúath** have bred new tribes — smaller, meaner, and more cunning than before.

They no longer march beneath a single banner but thrive in shadows and ruins:

- In the **Grey Mountains**, they raid Dwarven mines and settlements.
- In the **Eastemnet**, roving warbands ambush traders and travelers.
- Deep beneath **Mount Gundabad**, something ancient stirs — a warlord or spirit uniting the tribes once more.

Most Men believe these are isolated threats. The wise know better.

---

## ⚖️ The Current Age

The **Fourth Age of Middle-earth** is an age of quiet decay — not of doom, but of forgetting. The great kingdoms of Men squabble and wane. The Elves are but memory. The Dwarves dig deep, and the Hobbits hide.

The world is no longer divided between good and evil, but between **those who remember** and **those who have forgotten**. The ruins of the Third Age lie silent across the land, waiting for those with courage — or folly — enough to unearth them.

> _The Shadow no longer has a name. But it has not vanished._
